Critique of University of North Carolina at Wilmington's Computing Resource Use Policy

Carl M. Kadie, Ph.D.
kadie@eff.org
Computer and Academic Freedom Archive
http://www.eff.org/CAF/ 

The computer policy of UNC at Wilmington has been in the news recently. UNCW professor Mike Adams is accused by a student's family of violating this policy in his reply to student's political email. For details of the case see

  • http://www.thefire.org/, http://www.thefire.org/issues/uncw_122001.php3, http://www.thefire.org/issues/rosa.php3, http://www.thefire.org/issues/uncw_121801.php3, http://www.politechbot.com/p-02961.html, http://www.politechbot.com/p-02966.html, http://www.politechbot.com/p-02958.html, http://www.politechbot.com/p-02957.html

The policy can be found at http://www.uncwil.edu/dcs/information/advisory.htm and as an appendix at the end of this critique. You are encouraged to read the policy before reading this critique.

This critique does not address the question of whether Professor Adams's reply violates this policy. Rather, the critique looks at how consistent the policy is with moral and legal principles of academic freedom, due process, privacy, and freedom of expression. Also, this critique covers both students and faculty, not just faculty.

Summary

It's likely that a great deal of work went into the creation of this policy. The policy, however, shows very little knowledge of academic freedom, even in those cases where such respect is required by law and by other UNCW policies. Specifically, the policy allows for summary expulsion from the computer system with no due process or appeal procedure. The policy allows system administrators to snoop and disclose email in likely violation of the law and other UNCW policies. The policy imposes an Speech Code on computer speech, illegally censoring offensive speech. I strongly recommend the policy be revised by the Faculty and Student Senates.

Details

Here is a detailed critique of the policy starting with its title. My comments indented.

Computing Resource Use Policy

Policy Revision September 1996

This is a good title and it's nice that the policy includes a date. The policy doesn't, however, say how it was created. The implication is that it was created by the Office of Information Technology without review by the Student or Faculty Senates.

 According to the Joint Statement on Rights and Freedoms of Students: Disciplinary proceedings should be
instituted only for violations of standards of conduct formulated with significant student participation [...]

The Joint Statement on Rights and Freedoms of Students is not just the main statement of student academic freedom in the United States. It is also part of the UNC-Wilmington's official Student Code (http://www.uncwil.edu/stuaff/doso/code/code.pdf).

I'd suggest that Student and Faculty Senates revise the policy (with input from the Office of Information Technology and other members of the university community).

Policy Statement

[...] Access to the university’s computing facilities and resources [...] is a privilege granted to UNCW faculty, staff, and students. Users of these resources are responsible for reading and understanding this document.

The term "privilege" suggests that the University has authority to act arbitrarily and without due process, when in fact, it does not. Computer access should be treated like other campus resources with the same due process protections. See http://www.eff.org/CAF/faq/just-a-privilege.html for the legal details. (Disclaimer: I'm not a lawyer.)

UNCW equipment may be monitored to insure the safety, operating performance, and patterns of usage of the equipment. Therefore, users do not have an expectation of privacy regarding their uses of the system.

So, student working as a part time computer admin is allowed to browse faculty files? So, any human subject data on the computer can be published to the web?

It does not logically follow that because UNCW equipment is monitored, users cannot expect privacy. Indeed, UNCW already has a policy that balances the needs of routine access and maintenance with the need for privacy: That would be it's dorm privacy policy (found in the Student Code). That policy respects student privacy while allowing for "routine maintenance". It also give procedures for searches in response to a warrant. (For examples of some good computer privacy policies from other universities see http://www.eff.org/CAF/faq/email.policies.)

Ethically (and perhaps legally) email communications should have the same privacy protection as telephone calls. It would be unwise for any university employee to tap email communications without authorization from the university president, university legal counsel, and the academic freedom committee. The laws that may protect university include the Electronic Communications Privacy Act (ECPA), the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (when students are involved) and the 1st, 4th, and 5th Amendments to the U.S. Constitution. See http://www.eff.org/CAF/faq/email.privacy.

Finally, this policy contradicts UNCW's own staff Computer Ethics policy which requires that staff must be concerned about the "right to privacy" (see http://www.uncwil.edu/sp/admproc/Msc100.htm#Computing_Ethics)

 [...]. Further, information contained on UNCW equipment and in UNCW accounts, including e-mail, if "made or received pursuant to law or ordinance in connection with the transaction of public business by any agency of North Carolina", unless subject to specific statutory exceptions and exemptions, may be subject to inspection under the Public Records Law of the State of North Carolina.

If North Carolina law doesn't give faculty the right to private correspondence, it needs to be changed. For example, I'm sure many UNCW professors are reviewer for journals and conferences. Under North Carolina law and this computer policy are confidential reviews now public? If so, I don't see how any UNCW professor could participate in peer-reviewed scholarship..

UNCW resources are provided for university employment, research, and academic purposes. Although occasional, brief personal uses are permissible, such as on lunch or other breaks by employees, or for family communication by students, such uses are only permissible if:

It's good that personal use is acknowledged. But what would you think if the University Library had an analogous policy that allowed recreational reading but only with strong restrictions.

  • [...]
  • the use does not overload UNCW systems or otherwise harm or negatively impact system performance;
  • [...]

Since *any* use of a shared computer or network negatively impacts system performance this could be used to punish any personal use. Since almost every computer user makes some personal use of computers, this could be used to punish almost anyone.

I'd suggest three improvements this part of the policy:

1. Acknowledge  the importance of self-directed projects. UNCW's own student code says: "The university therefore supports and encourages freedom of inquiry for faculty members and students, to the end that they responsibly pursue these goals through teaching, learning, research, discussion and publication, free from internal or external restraints that would unreasonably  restrict their academic endeavors." (The Faculty Handbook has a similar statement, http://www.uncwil.edu/fac_handbook/.) The Joint Statement says: "As members of the academic community, students should be encouraged to develop the capacity for critical judgment and to engage in a sustained and independent search for truth." Note that activities once considered frivolous uses of computer resources, for example, computer games are now important industries and increasingly important art forms.

2. Change the standard from "negatively impact" to "substantially disrupt", the standard given for academic free expression in the Supreme Court's Tinker decision (see http://www.eff.org/CAF/law/tinker_v_des_moines)

3. Finally, introducing the notion of priorities. While sending personal email, creating and playing games, etc. might be allowed and encouraged, it might also have less priority than a home work assignment due tomorrow.

    [...]The university recognizes the value and potential of publishing on the Internet, and so allows and encourages students, staff, and faculty to do so consistent with this policy. Contents of all electronic pages, including direct links to other pages or computers, must be consistent with university policies and local, state, and federal laws. The university does allow for personal pages that provide information about an individual that is relevant to that individual’s role at the university. Links from the official UNCW home pages will be made for faculty, staff, and registered student organizations. The campus home pages will not link directly to individual students’ personal pages. Users may, if they choose, link to other faculty, staff, or student personal pages.
As a condition of maintaining personal pages on university systems or equipment, the following statement will appear on all personal pages: "The views and opinions expressed in this page are strictly those of the page author. The contents of this page have not been reviewed or approved by the University of North Carolina at Wilmington."

Generally very good, but what does it mean that all pages directly linked must be consistent with university policy? Does this mean that outside pages must include the "These contents have not been reviewed by UNCW" disclaimer? If not, exactly which internal policies must an external page follow? Does UNCW have a similar rule for references in the academic papers? Are student and faculty forbidden from referencing other papers that don't meet UNCW policy?

Guidelines

Conduct which violates this policy includes, but is not limited to, activities on the following list:

1. Unauthorized use of a computer account. Do not try to obtain a password for someone else’s computer account or share a computer account. Do not attempt to disguise the identity of the account or machine you are using. Do not attempt to forge or conceal the source (sender, user, or host computer) of any communication, transmission, or computer access.

Although fraud is illegal, anonymous speech is Constitutionally protected. The Supreme Court has said:  "Anonymously pamphlets, leaflets, brochures and even books have played an important role in the progress of mankind. Persecuted groups and sects from time to time throughout history have been able to criticize oppressive practices and laws either anonymously or not at all."
Tally v. California, 362 U.S. 60, 64 (1960) (See http://www.eff.org/CAF/law/talley-v-california and http://www.eff.org/CAF/law/talley-v-california)

[...] 4. Unauthorized attempts to circumvent data protection schemes or uncover security loopholes. This includes creating and/or running programs that are designed to identify security loopholes and /or decrypt intentionally secure data.

Looking for security loopholes is a valid academic pursuit. The policy needs a procedure to allow it.

5. Knowingly or carelessly performing an act that will interfere with the normal operation of computers, terminals, peripherals, or networks. This includes running or installing on any computer system or network, or giving to another user a program intended to damage or to place excessive load on a computer system or network (such as computer viruses). Tampering with components, blocking communication lines, or interfering with the operational readiness of a computer. Deliberately wasting/overloading computing resources, such as printing too many copies of a document.
In addition, inappropriate mass mailings, including multiple mailings to newsgroups, mailing lists, or individuals (e.g. spamming, flooding, or bombing). Initiating, executing, or propagating cancel-bots (messages that delete other messages) or electronic chain letters is also prohibited.

This seems pretty good.

6. Violating terms of applicable software licensing agreements. In particular, you must not copy or distribute copyrighted or site-licensed software.

This is just confused. Copyrighted is not the same as licensed. There is nothing wrong with distributing copyrighted software if, as in the case of GNU or "Berkeley" software, the copyright owner allows it.

7. Reproducing and/or distributing copyrighted materials without proper authorization. Be sure you have permission to publish the information, graphics, or photographs on your pages if you are not the author or creator. Photographs of an individual or personal information about an individual must not be included without the permission of the individual. E-mail messages, unless otherwise indicated in the text, or by special permission of the author, may also be subject to copyright, and should not be forwarded without the permission of the author.

I think this is generally good, but it neglects fair use. This is a complicated legal topic. I wonder if it can be addressed adequately in a computer policy.

8. Displaying obscene, illegal, or sexually harassing images, sounds, or text in a public computer facility or location that is in view of others.

There is no such thing as an inherently "sexually harassing image, sound, or text". Almost any image, sound, or text could be used a person to sexually harass another person. Likewise most all of those images, sounds, and text could also be used for constitutionally-protected purposes. So, how can you distinguish them? Not with one line in a computer policy. Rather you need a detailed due process procedure. UNCW has such a policy in it's sexual harassment policy. I suggest that the computer policy just refer to that policy. See http://www.eff.org/CAF/faq/censorship-and-harassment.html and http://www.eff.org/CAF/faq/computer-porn)

9. Transmitting or reproducing materials that are libelous or defamatory in nature. This includes, but is not limited to, information or images that infringe upon the rights of another person, that is abusive or threatening, profane, or is sexually, racially, or religiously offensive to the average person. Information that is libelous is defined as provably false, unprivileged statements that do demonstrated injury to an individual’s or a business’ reputation.

This rule redefines two types of generally protected speech as unprotected and then censors them. "Profane" means vulgar or irreverent to what is sacred. It has nothing to do with libel or defamation. Profane speech is generally protected by the First Amendment.  Likewise, offensive speech (sexual, religious, racial, or otherwise) is exactly what the First Amendment, academic freedom, and freedom of expression protect. There is no need to protect inoffensive speech. The opinion of the "average person" as to what is profane or offensive is irrelevant.

The courts have found that it is illegal for state universities to ban speech merely because it is "offensive". (see http://www.eff.org/CAF/faq/censorship-and-harassment). It is also a violation of UNCW's on principles as expressed in the Student Code and the Faculty Handbook.

[...] 11. Creating pages which contain direct links to pages that violate this policy. In this case, users may be requested to deactivate links to materials that violate this policy.

This rule was already listed earlier in the policy. I don't know why it is listed twice. See the critique above.

[...]

Enforcement

Misuse of computing, networking, or information resources may result in the loss of computing and/or network access. Any conduct which violates local, state, or federal laws will result in the immediate loss of all university computing privileges and will be referred to appropriate university offices and/or law enforcement authorities.

"No, no," said the Queen: "The sentence first -- the verdict afterwards." -- Lewis Carroll, Alice in Wonderland

If the Office of Information Technology  wants to follow the it's university's own rules, basic principles of fairness, and the law, it should not impose punishments until after it determined that a computer user actually violated a rule.

According to the UNCW Student Code: "In applying regulations in the area of student discipline, each constituent institution shall adhere to the requirements of due process as set forth in Section 502D(3) of this Code." Students can only be punished after due process. The possible punishments are Oral Reprimand, Written Reprimand, Disciplinary Probation, Suspension, & Indefinite Suspension, Expulsion (only in the case of drug offenses). Note that loss of computer access, is not listed.

But what about emergencies? Student Code does have provisions for summary suspensions, but only "when there is reasonable cause to believe, based on available facts, that the student is an immediate threat to the safety of himself, or herself, other members of the university community or university property, or is disruptive without relief to the university community." And only according to a set procedure.

For more details see http://www.eff.org/CAF/faq/due-process.html

The policy could be improved by being made consistent with the law on due process and the principles of academic freedom.
 

Conclusion

Computers are not just another piece of lab equipment. They are now a primary tool in the academic mission. They are critical to research and communications. For more and more members of the University community, and with the planned rise in distance learning, the computer is the University. the University of North Carolina at Wilmington deserves a great computer policy.

I'm not a lawyer. I don't speak for EFF or my employer.

Appendix

[A copy of the policy. It is also available at http://www.uncwil.edu/dcs/information/advisory.htm]

Computing Resource Use Policy

Policy Revision September 1996

Policy Statement

The University of North Carolina at Wilmington (UNCW), through the Office of Information Technology, provides central computing facilities and services for the instructional, research, and administrative computing needs of the university. Access to the university’s computing facilities and resources, which include but are not limited to all CPUs, online and offline storage, and network and communications facilities, is a privilege granted to UNCW faculty, staff, and students. Users of these resources are responsible for reading and understanding this document.
UNCW equipment may be monitored to insure the safety, operating performance, and patterns of usage of the equipment. Therefore, users do not have an expectation of privacy regarding their uses of the system, and the issuance of confidential passwords or specific addresses should not be understood to provide an expectation of privacy. Further, information contained on UNCW equipment and in UNCW accounts, including e-mail, if "made or received pursuant to law or ordinance in connection with the transaction of public business by any agency of North Carolina", unless subject to specific statutory exceptions and exemptions, may be subject to inspection under the Public Records Law of the State of North Carolina.
UNCW resources are provided for university employment, research, and academic purposes. Although occasional, brief personal uses are permissible, such as on lunch or other breaks by employees, or for family communication by students, such uses are only permissible if:

  • the use is lawful under state and federal law;
  • the use is not prohibited by any Board of Governors, UNC, or UNCW policies;
  • the use does not harm or invade the rights of others;
  • the use does not overload UNCW systems or otherwise harm or negatively impact system performance;
  • the use does not state or imply university sponsorship or endorsement;
  • the use does not result in commercial gain or private profit (other than allowable under intellectual property policies or other applicable university policies or officially sanctioned practices);
  • the use does not interfere or conflict with the performance of academic or university assignments, duties, and work.

Users are responsible for all use of their computer accounts; therefore, they should make appropriate use of the system-provided protection features and take precautions against others obtaining access to their computer resources. Users will only use their accounts for the purpose for which they are approved, and accounts are not transferable. Users will not compromise the university systems at any time by sharing their accounts with other users, on- or off-campus; nor will they attempt to subvert other systems from the university.
The university recognizes the value and potential of publishing on the Internet, and so allows and encourages students, staff, and faculty to do so consistent with this policy. Contents of all electronic pages, including direct links to other pages or computers, must be consistent with university policies and local, state, and federal laws. The university does allow for personal pages that provide information about an individual that is relevant to that individual’s role at the university. Links from the official UNCW home pages will be made for faculty, staff, and registered student organizations. The campus home pages will not link directly to individual students’ personal pages. Users may, if they choose, link to other faculty, staff, or student personal pages.
As a condition of maintaining personal pages on university systems or equipment, the following statement will appear on all personal pages: "The views and opinions expressed in this page are strictly those of the page author. The contents of this page have not been reviewed or approved by the University of North Carolina at Wilmington."

Guidelines

Conduct which violates this policy includes, but is not limited to, activities on the following list:

1. Unauthorized use of a computer account. Do not try to obtain a password for someone else’s computer account or share a computer account. Do not attempt to disguise the identity of the account or machine you are using. Do not attempt to forge or conceal the source (sender, user, or host computer) of any communication, transmission, or computer access.

2. Use of the campus network to gain unauthorized access to remote computers.

3. Accessing directories, programs, files, or data not belonging to you unless there is specific authorization to do so or the information is meant to be publicly available.

4. Unauthorized attempts to circumvent data protection schemes or uncover security loopholes. This includes creating and/or running programs that are designed to identify security loopholes and /or decrypt intentionally secure data.

5. Knowingly or carelessly performing an act that will interfere with the normal operation of computers, terminals, peripherals, or networks. This includes running or installing on any computer system or network, or giving to another user a program intended to damage or to place excessive load on a computer system or network (such as computer viruses). Tampering with components, blocking communication lines, or interfering with the operational readiness of a computer. Deliberately wasting/overloading computing resources, such as printing too many copies of a document.
In addition, inappropriate mass mailings, including multiple mailings to newsgroups, mailing lists, or individuals (e.g. spamming, flooding, or bombing). Initiating, executing, or propagating cancel-bots (messages that delete other messages) or electronic chain letters is also prohibited.

6. Violating terms of applicable software licensing agreements. In particular, you must not copy or distribute copyrighted or site-licensed software.

7. Reproducing and/or distributing copyrighted materials without proper authorization. Be sure you have permission to publish the information, graphics, or photographs on your pages if you are not the author or creator. Photographs of an individual or personal information about an individual must not be included without the permission of the individual. E-mail messages, unless otherwise indicated in the text, or by special permission of the author, may also be subject to copyright, and should not be forwarded without the permission of the author.

8. Displaying obscene, illegal, or sexually harassing images, sounds, or text in a public computer facility or location that is in view of others.

9. Transmitting or reproducing materials that are libelous or defamatory in nature. This includes, but is not limited to, information or images that infringe upon the rights of another person, that is abusive or threatening, profane, or is sexually, racially, or religiously offensive to the average person. Information that is libelous is defined as provably false, unprivileged statements that do demonstrated injury to an individual’s or a business’ reputation.

10. Using university or campus logos, wordmarks, service marks, or other symbols of the university or campus on personal home pages.

11. Creating pages which contain direct links to pages that violate this policy. In this case, users may be requested to deactivate links to materials that violate this policy.

12. Failure to abide by the current specific account usage Rules that are maintained on the VAXcluster. These Rules are viewed by entering the command RULES from within your user account.

Enforcement

Misuse of computing, networking, or information resources may result in the loss of computing and/or network access. Any conduct which violates local, state, or federal laws will result in the immediate loss of all university computing privileges and will be referred to appropriate university offices and/or law enforcement authorities. Users will also be held accountable for their conduct under all applicable university policies and procedures. Violation of this policy is an offense under the Code of Student Life, is "misconduct" and "unacceptable personal conduct" under applicable personnel policies, for which dismissal or discharge proceedings may be appropriate, and may also constitute a criminal violation under Chapter 14, Article 60, of the North Carolina General Statutes.
