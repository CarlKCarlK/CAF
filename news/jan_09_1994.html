Abstract

The words after the numbers are a short PARAPHRASES of the articles, or QUOTES from them, NOT AN OBJECTIVE SUMMARY and not necessarily my opinion.

  1. Censorship of Fanny Hill (was Re: Unsuitable upload: please delete) 
        [paraphrase: [The archivist of the CICA archive at Indiana University:]
    The on-line version of _Fanny Hill_ has been removed from the archive.
    "The reason for removing it [... was] simple: Indiana University is a
    state-owned and state-funded institution that does not support
    pornographic/adult/salacious/licentious activities."
  2. ADMIN: shutdown of rec.arts.erotica mailing list and file server 
        [Summary of an article from last October: The mailing list reflector
    for rec.arts.erotica at the Computer Science department of U. of
    Illinois has been shut down. It has been set up by a department sys
    admin. It was shut down by the department on the grounds that it "is
    not a necessary allocation of the resources involved."]
  3. Computer Users' Privileges and Responsibilities, Indiana U. 
        [paraphase: A policy critique. Participation in policy making is good.
    Privacy protection is perhaps the best I've ever seen. Due process
    protection is good except that the policy seems to authorize users to
    be punished with a loss of computer access without due process. Free
    expression protection is poor. The policy likely violates the law (and
    the principles of academic freedom) by authorizing sys admin to censor
    "bigoted" speech. Other provisions of the "free expression" section
    are so vague that no one could tell if he or she is subject to
    censorship or not.]
  4. CICA and Ignorance 
        [paraphrase: The CICA archivist explains in several articles why
    _Fanny Hill_ was excluded. Enclosed is a list of those
    explanations.  The gist seems to be that the CICA archive excludes
    even Windows-specific material such as the hypertext _Fanny Hill_
    because of fears that the University would consider sexual subject
    matter unacceptable.  This reason violates intellectual freedom
    principles.]
  5. CICA Removed Fanny Hill (was Re: Censorship of Fanny Hill...) 
        [paraphrase: Contrary to stated policy, the CICA archive contains
    other "adult" material. I found a passage about a "whore" whose lovers
    were hung like donkeys and who ejaculated like horses. Other "adult"
    passages are enclosed. The source? the CICA archives's on-line
    versions of the Bible.]
  6. CICA Removed Fanny Hill (was Re: Censorship of Fanny Hill...) 
        [paraphrase: Not all reason for excluding or removing material are
    equivalent. "Deleting a file for lack of space does not have the same
    censorship significance as deleting a file because its content may be
    offensive to some."]
  7. CICA Removed Fanny Hill (was Re: Censorship of Fanny Hill...) 
        [paraphrase: Here is an overview of the legal argument against the
    exclusion/removal: The First Amendment applies to the states, to state
    universities, and to state university employees when they are acting
    within the scope of their employement. In the _Island Tree v. Pico_
    (1982) Supreme Court case '"four of the nine justices said that
    school boards may not remove books from the school library shelves
    simply because they dislike the (sexual) ideas contained in those
    books." (4 said no. 1 didn't say.)']
  8. CICA and Ignorance 
        [parphrase: The moral argument against the exclusion (as contrasted
    with the legal argument) says that "exclusion merely on the basis of
    sexual content is [...] wrong." (Not all censorship is illegal. Some
    is legal, but wrong.)]
  9. CICA Removed Fanny Hill (was Re: Censorship of Fanny Hill...) 
        [paraphrase: A previous article said a university should not select
    material for a general archive on the basis of what is appropriate for
    grade school children. I agree. But Indiana U. 'does not put "adult"
    material into an archive that can be accessed by minors. Period.'
    (According the circulation desk at the Indiana U. library, you must be
    16 years or older and have a driver's license or other ID to use the
    collection.) American Library Association recommendations can "not
    negate local laws".]
  10. CICA Removed Fanny Hill (was Re: Censorship of Fanny Hill...) 
        [paraphrase: A reply to the claim that 'IU does not provide
    "adult" materials to minors'. A 16-year old is a minor so the
    claim is false. Also, even though "a 10 year old is not allowed to

    photocopy a book.]
  11. University staff on their own time (was CICA Removed Fanny Hill) 
        [paraphrase: Can university computer staff run an archive or
    mailing list on their own, not as state agents? I think yes, but
    to avoid messy situations, there should be a policy on staff and
    student mailing lists and archives. A good model might be the
    rules for student/staff organizations.]
  12. Fear of institutional retaliation (was CICA Removed Fanny Hill)

ADMIN: shutdown of rec.arts.erotica mailing list and file server

4 Oct 1993 18:55:46 -0400
Newsgroups:
rec.arts.erotica 
Archive-name: admin-10-93.2

Joshua Laff has informed me that as of noon today (Monday, 4 Oct 1993)
the e-mail file server and mailing list reflector for rec.arts.erotica
have been shut down.  

This is the notice he's posted regarding the matter:

   This service has been permanently discontinuted. The CS department at
   the University feels that the material distributed from this service
   is not a necessary allocation of the resources involved. Please do not
   ask for any more information regarding this matter.

                                                -Josh Laff

Josh and I will be happy to cooperate with anyone who has the time,
interest and ability to make this service available on their machine.
The requirements are:

   1) access to rec.arts.erotica, either from a news directory or NNTP
   2) ability to make use of a crontab file
   3) ability to access a mail handler on the system

Apparently it does not have a very deleterious effect on system
performance.  I would be interested in preparing the service for this
machine if I weren't already spending time enough as it is moderating
the newsgroup.

I apologize for the inconvenience.

-- 
Moderator, rec.arts.erotica.  Submissions to erotica@unix.amherst.edu.
Please, no reposts, first drafts, or requests for "subscriptions,"
stories, GIFs, or archive sites.

Re: Computer Users' Privileges and Responsibilities, Indiana U.

kadie@cs.uiuc.edu (Carl M Kadie)
Sat, 8 Jan 1994 08:07:50 GMT
Newsgroups:
comp.admin.policy, news, admin.policy, alt.comp.acad-freedom.talk 
This is a critique. See the references for a whole archive
of other academic policies and critiques.

Summary: Participation in policy making is good. Privacy protection is
perhaps the best I've ever seen. Due process protection is good except
that the policy seems to authorize users to be punished with a loss of
computer access without due process. Free expression protection is
poor. The policy likely violates the law (and the principles of
academic freedom) by authorizing sys admin to censor "bigoted" speech.
Other provisions of the "free expression" section are so vague that no
one tell if he or she is subject to censorship or not.

=======================
>From "gopher -p1/help/policies gopher.indiana.edu 70"
> --> Computer Use Ethics (IU Policy)
[...]
>               Computer Users' Privileges and Responsibilities
>          Approved by the Academic Computing Policy Committee, 1990
[...]
>   Whether you're reprinting this publication, have a comment
>   about it, or would like more information on this or other UCS
>   publications, we'd like to hear from you at newdesk@ucs.indiana.edu.

Excellent. This shows that the policy was created, as it should
be, with the participation of the university community.

[...]
>      Confidentiality [...]

>      IU computing centers will maintain the confidentiality of all
>      information stored on their computing resources. Similarly,
>      privileged information on account usage (i.e. that available
>      only to users with system privileges) will be held in
>      confidence.
>
>      Requests for disclosure of confidential information will be
>      reviewed by the administrator of the computer system involved.
>      Such requests will be honored only when approved by University
>      officials authorized by the of the campus involved, or when
>      required by state or federal law. Except when inappropriate,
>      computer users will receive prior notice of such disclosures.

Excellent. I don't recall ever seeing an explicit promise of
confidentiality for privileged usage information.

>      Censorship
>
>      Free expression of ideas is central to the academic process.

Excellent. This statement could be improved by mentioning that free
access to ideas is also central to the academic process.

>      IU computer system administrators will not remove any
>      information from individual accounts or from electronic
>      bulletin boards maintained on them [...] unless the appropriate
>      system administrator finds that:
>
>      The presence of the information on the bulletin board involves
>      illegality (e.g. copyrighted material, software used in violation
>      of a license agreement).

Since not all postings of copyrighted material are illegal (for
example, GNU material is copyrighted and legal to post), this could be
improved by replacing "copyrighted material" with "posted in violation
of copyright".

>      The information in some way endangers computing resources or the
>      information of other users (e.g. a computer worm, virus, or other
>      destructive program).

A rule is unconstitutionally vague when people "of common intelligence
must necessarily guess at its meaning" [see references]. I believe
this rule is unconstitutionally. It could be read to prohibit the
posting of a research paper about computer viruses. I suggest adopting
the Supreme Court's standard (from _Brandenburg v. Ohio_). Material is
protected unless it is intended to produce 'imminent lawless action'
and it is 'likely to produce such action.' You may also want to adapt
the university's policy on "disruptive demonstrations", if it has one.

>      The information is inappropriate, because it is unrelated to or
>      is inconsistent with the mission of the University,

This is too vague, perhaps unconstitutionally vague.

>      involves the use of obscene, bigoted, or abusive language on
>      bulletin boards on IU resources,

This is too broad. I believe it is illegal for a state university to
ban "bigoted" speech, even on IU resources [see references]. This
prohibition may render this whole policy section void (as did a
similar prohibitions at U. of Wisconsin and U. of Michigan).

Also, many, including the American Association of University
Professors, that such bans violate important academic freedom
principles [see references]. The University Senate should at least
have a chance to debate if they want a hate speech code on campus.

>      [...] Users whose information is removed will be notified of
>      the removal as soon as is feasible. Users who wish to appeal
>      such removal of information may do so through an appeals board
>      made up of the governing body appropriate to the status of the
>      user.

It is good that some due process is provided for. I think due process
could be better protected if removals and bans had to be approved
by a university officer and not just a sys admin acting on
his or own.

> RESPONSIBILITIES OF THE USER

>      All classes of computer service user (faculty, staff, and
>      students) may use computing resources only for purposes related
>      to their studies, their instruction, the discharge of their
>      duties as employees, their official business with the University,
>      and their other University-sanctioned activities.

This seems to prohibit most personal e-mail. Does it? How does this
compare to the university's policy on incidental personal use of
telephones?

[...]
>      >  Intentional harassment of other users.
[...]

I believe the phrase "Intentional harassment of other users" is
redundant. I suggest replacing with the single word "harassment".

[...]
> SANCTIONS

>      Violation of the policies described above for legal and ethical
>      use of computing resources will be dealt with seriously.
>      Violators will be subject to the normal disciplinary procedures
>      of the University and, in addition, the loss of computing
>      privileges may result.

I suggest changing 
    "and, in addition, the loss of computing privileges may result"
 to 
    ", which may result in, among other possible sanctions, suspension of
    computing privileges".
The rewording makes clear that the loss-of-computing-privileges
sanction will be the result of due process rather than the result of a
summary decision.
[...]

- Carl

=================
law/broadrick-v-oklahoma
=================
* Expression -- Vague Regulation -- Broadrick v. Oklahoma, et al.

Summary of case law on overly vague regulation of expression. It says
a statute is unconstitutionally vague when "men of common intelligence
must necessarily guess at its meaning."

=================
law/naacp-v-button
=================
* Expression -- Overbroad Regulation -- NAACP v. Button, et al.

Summary of case law on overly broad regulation of expression. It says
"[b]ecause First Amendment freedoms need breathing space to survive,
government may regulate in the area only with narrow specificity."

=================
law/brandenburg-v-ohio
=================
* Expression -- Advocating Lawbreaking -- Brandenberg v. Ohio

In e-mail, a correspondent expressed the view that there was no right
to speech that advocated violence. This response is based on U.S. law.
It is a summary of the ACLU's Bill of Rights Briefing Paper #10:
Freedom of Expression. The Supreme Court's standard is that speech may
not be suppressed or punished unless it is intended to produce
'imminent lawless action' and it is 'likely to produce such action.'

=================
law/uwm-post-v-u-of-wisconsin
=================
* Expression -- Hate Speech -- UWM Post v. U Of Wisconsin

The full text of UWM POST v. U. of Wisconsin. This recent district
court ruling goes into detail about the difference between protected
offensive expression and illegal harassment. It even mentions email.

It concludes: "The founding fathers of this nation produced a
remarkable document in the Constitution but it was ratified only with
the promise of the Bill of Rights.  The First Amendment is central to
our concept of freedom.  The God-given "unalienable rights" that the
infant nation rallied to in the Declaration of Independence can be
preserved only if their application is rigorously analyzed.

The problems of bigotry and discrimination sought to be addressed here
are real and truly corrosive of the educational environment.  But
freedom of speech is almost absolute in our land and the only
restriction the fighting words doctrine can abide is that based on the
fear of violent reaction.  Content-based prohibitions such as that in
the UW Rule, however well intended, simply cannot survive the
screening which our Constitution demands."

=================
law/doe-v-u-of-michigan
=================
* Expression -- Hate Speech -- Doe v. U of Michigan

This is Doe v. University of Michigan. In this widely referenced
decision, the district judge down struck the University's rules
against discriminatory harassment because the rules were found to be too
broad and too vague.

On Freedom of Expression and Campus Speech Codes Expression - An

Comments from _A Practical Guide to Legal Issues Affecting College

law/doe-v-u-of-michigan
=================
* Expression -- Hate Speech -- Doe v. U of Michigan

This is Doe v. University of Michigan. In this widely referenced
decision, the district judge down struck the University's rules
against discriminatory harassment because the rules were found to be too
broad and too vague.

=================
law/keyishian-v-board-of-regents
=================
* Constitution -- Public University -- Keyishian v. Board Of Regents

In this Supreme Court case, the Court said that public universities
can not infringe on the Constitutionally protected rights of their
students and employees (specially with regard to loyalty oaths).

=================
law/student-publications.misc
=================
* Expression -- Offensive -- Student Publications -- Misc

Quotes from the book _Law of the Student Press_ by the Student Press
Law Center (1985,1988). They say that four-letter words are protected
speech, that public universities are not likely to be liable for
publications that they for which they do not control the contents, and
that the _Hazelwood_ decision does not apply to universities.

=================
law/widmar-v-vincent
=================
* Expression -- On Campus -- Widmar v. Vincent

An excerpt from _The Rights of Religious Persons in Public Education_ by
John W. Whitehead summarizing how the Supreme Court case of Widmar v. Vincent
established that public university campuses are limited public forums.

=================
law/young-conservatives-v-sau
=================
* Expression -- Offensive -- Young Conservatives v. SAU

A UPI story that tells how Stephen F. Austin University originally
banned a group's "sexist" flyers, but when challenged, the ban was
lifted and a cash settlement was given to the students whose
free-speech was violated by the ban.

=================
law/uwm-post-v-u-of-wisconsin
=================
* Expression -- Hate Speech -- UWM Post v. U Of Wisconsin

The full text of UWM POST v. U. of Wisconsin. This recent district
court ruling goes into detail about the difference between protected
offensive expression and illegal harassment. It even mentions email.

It concludes: "The founding fathers of this nation produced a
remarkable document in the Constitution but it was ratified only with
the promise of the Bill of Rights.  The First Amendment is central to
our concept of freedom.  The God-given "unalienable rights" that the
infant nation rallied to in the Declaration of Independence can be
preserved only if their application is rigorously analyzed.

The problems of bigotry and discrimination sought to be addressed here
are real and truly corrosive of the educational environment.  But
freedom of speech is almost absolute in our land and the only
restriction the fighting words doctrine can abide is that based on the
fear of violent reaction.  Content-based prohibitions such as that in
the UW Rule, however well intended, simply cannot survive the
screening which our Constitution demands."
