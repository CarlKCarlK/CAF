Jensen won't deny lab incident was a set-up
Lab assistant Gary Stewart says Hitler website was not an issue in expelling student from lab

By GLENN HALTERMAN
JOURNAL EDITOR

A story that has garnered nationwide attention the past week has taken a
twist with the discovery of new information that may shed some additional
light on the issue.

On Thursday, Feb. 18, the Journal printed a story about Michaun Jensen,
a junior sociology/psychology major from Boulder City, Nev., who was
allegedly kicked out of an ELC computer lab after accessing an Internet web
sites with pornography and another about former Nazi leader Adolf Hitler.

At that time, the ELC lab assistant, Gary Stewart, whom Jensen said told
her that she would have to leave the lab, was unable to be reached for
comment on the matter.

Now, Stewart, a junior CIS networking major from St. George, has had the
opportunity to tell his version of events from the now infamous evening of
Feb. 9.

Stewart said that he was approached by a student who told him that
Jensen was looking at pornography. Stewart said he then went to Jensen and
told her that a student had complained about her looking at pornography.

Stewart said that Jensen asked him what could happen to her if she
continued to look at the pornography.

Stewart said he told her that if she didn't stop he could kick her out
of the lab.

Stewart said that about 20 minutes later he was approached by another
male student who had been previously talking with Jensen. He said that the
student told him that Jensen was still looking at objectionable material on
her computer, though he did not specifically mention pornography.

Stewart also said that the student told him, "She [Jensen] wants you to
kick her out," adding, "Don't tell her I told you this."

Jensen claims not to know the person that was talking to her in the lab,
though she said that she has seen him at school.

"It's a small campus," she said.

"I was thinking that this was an assignment for a class," Stewart, a
psychology minor, said, adding that he is aware that professors in the
social sciences sometimes have their students do things against social norms
to see what others' reactions will be.

He said he then talked to Jensen for the second time.

"You've still been looking at stuff you ought not to," Stewart said he
told her. "But not this," he added, referring to the picture of Hitler on
her computer screen.

Stewart said that Jensen then asked him, "So, you're kicking me out?"

Stewart said he replied, "Sure," at which point Jensen had him sign a
paper stating "You have to leave."

"The only two reasons I kicked her out were primarily, because she
wanted to be and secondly, for the pornography," Stewart said. He also said
that he wouldn't have expelled her from the lab for looking at a website
about Hitler.

"I was just helping her pass her class," he said.

Stewart said that he has had other people in the ELC computer lab looking
at pornography. However, he said that if they are in their own corner and
are trying to be discreet, he leaves them alone.

However, Jensen expressed sentiments indicating that she did not agree
with Stewart's account of events. "I don't know where his story is coming
from," she said.

Jensen would not deny that what occurred in the computer lab was a
set-up, either. "I don't think that is relevant," she said, explaining that
her being asked to leave could have happened whether the episode in the lab
was a set-up or not.

When asked again whether the situation in the ELC was a set-up, Jensen
replied, "That's all I have to say."

"What I wanted to happen, happened," Jensen said. "They are reviewing
the policy."

"I wasn't supposed to go in there and get kicked out," she said.

"I'm not doing this as a personal vendetta. I like SUU as a school, I just feel they have a bad computer policy," Jensen said.
