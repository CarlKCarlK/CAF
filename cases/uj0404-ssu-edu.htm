Computer use policy garners national coverage

"Confessions of a Civilly Disobedient Student" posted on Computers and Academic Freedom Project web site


By GLENN HALTERMAN
JOURNAL EDITOR

It's probably safe to say that SUU could have emerged from relative
educational obscurity in more administration-friendly ways (such as a cancer
cure or a Cinderella ride to the Sweet 16 in this year's NCAA basketball
tournament).

However, SUU entered the consciousness of America with on-campus
developments in the last few weeks in the debate over proper computer usage
and the university's Acceptable Use Policy.

Most recently, the New York Times web-edition published a story about
Michaun Jensen and her Feb. 9 expulsion from an ELC computer lab. The piece
described the incident in the lab and also explored the problems other
university campuses are encountering with their own computer-use policies.

The Salt Lake Tribune also ran an article recently about the issue,
which included the Journal's photo of the Socrates statue, which was
blindfolded in protest of SUU's Acceptable Use Policy Feb. 19.

Other Internet information sources have also picked up the story,
including Wired and the Chronicle of Higher Education.

The Internet has also been a hotbed of discussion on the computer lab
incident, with the Computers and Academic Freedom Project (located at
www.eff.org/CAF/) giving the incident top billing on its site. The site
includes reprints of Journal publications related to the story as well as a
critique of SUU's Acceptable Use Policy.

Also included on the site is a note from Jensen titled "Confessions of a
Civilly Disobedient Student." The note reads: "I confess that I 'tricked'
SUU into enforcing their vague and probably illegal computer policy.

I went to the computer lab and read material that I thought might
violate the policy. I then asked other students in the lab to denounce what
I was reading.

I did not know what punishment I might face. Under the policy almost
any punishment was possible.

The punishment I received was summary expulsion from the lab. The lab
assistant who expelled me now says that his was motivated only in part by my
choice of reading material. He says he also expelled me because he thought,
without me saying so to him, that I wanted to be expelled.

I did not want to be expelled just for the sake of expulsion. What I
wanted was to test the policy and demonstrate its unfairness. A speech code
that is never enforced can still censor by chilling speech. I hoped that the
heat from my civilly disobedient act would melt that chill."

The SUU administration has also been bombarded by e-mail from across the
country, much of it with scathing comments regarding First Amendment rights
and allusions to Hitler's Nazi regime.

One e-mail sent to President Steven D. Bennion asked, "Do you think the
SUU seal would look best with a swastika in the area of the shield, or
should it cover the entire seal?"

Another e-mail accused Bennion of having a "Big Brother mentality"
bringing "shame and disgrace upon the state of Utah." The same message
finishes, "May God bless Ms. Jensen, and may you fear facing your master who
inspired the Bill of Rights."
