[These are my comments on the remarks of D. Michael Carter, counsel to Southern Utah University, in the online New York Times article. - Carl Kadie.

The New York Times reports:

D. Michael Carter, counsel to the university, said officials were concerned, among other things, that the display of Internet pornography or other arguably offensive material could put the university at risk of liability in harassment or related litigation. "The so-called 'girlie calendars' have prompted sexual harassment complaints," he said in a telephone interview, adding that images on a computer screen "or live videos downloaded on a screen could only add fuel to a similar fire."

1.SUU already has a harassment policy that not only covers harassment via computers but that also dictates the exact procedure for handling sexual harassment complaints. Having a procedure is important because the line between constitutionally protected expression and unprotected expression is dim and uncertain. Computer sites should publicize the university's sexual harassment rules; they should not try to preempt or short circuit them.

Also, the federal courts have said 1. that EEOC rules that apply to employees in the workplace do not trump the constitutional rights of students at public universities. 2. that quiet reading of adult material by employees in the workplace is not harassment.

                 -- http://www.eff.org/CAF/faq/computer-porn.html
                 -- http://www.eff.org/CAF/faq/censorship-and-harassment.html

2. SUU policy goes well beyond stopping display of "arguably offensive material" in computer labs. It bans any acquisition and storage of "objectionable material" via the computer system even if that material is not displayed.. It is vague about due process, referring to users as "guests". It offers no privacy protection and implies that the university staff are authorized to read a professor's email or disclose a student's web browsing at will.

For a full critique of the policy, see  http://www.eff.org/CAF/policies/suu-edu.htm.

- Carl Kadie
