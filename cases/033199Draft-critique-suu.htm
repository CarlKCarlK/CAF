This is a critique of the 3/31/99 draft computer policy from Southern Utah University. The draft can be found at http://www.eff.org/CAF/cases/033199Draft-suu.htm.

- Carl Kadie

Summary

The policy is generally quite good. Minor changes in wording could make it "say what it means" better. One line seems to say that computer personnel can impose summary punishments. It should be replaced with a line that says that expulsion from the computer system is among the possible punishments for computer infractions. The policy also contains a strange rule against "violating mutual trust". This rule is vague and over-broad and should be removed.

Details

I. PURPOSE
To establish policy on acceptable use of Southern Utah University computer systems, networks, and Internet resources.

Because the policy now goes beyond "acceptable use", I suggest removing the word "acceptable":

"To establish policy on the use of Southern Utah University computer systems, networks, and Internet resources."

Or:

"To identify the rights and responsibilities of those who use Southern Utah University computer systems, networks, and Internet resources."

II. POLICY
Introduction
The computing systems at Southern Utah University are provided for the purposes of instruction, research, personal development, and administration. This policy informs users of the rights they may expect and their responsibilities. This policy applies to all users of SUU computing resources, including those who access these resources from off campus.

Excellent!

Rights
The University will provide an environment in which there is respect for freedom of inquiry and expression, opposition to censorship, privacy and confidentiality, freedom from sexual harassment, and protection of intellectual property. Respect for law, for due process, and the presumption of innocence are crucial elements of this environment.

The punctuation here needs to be tightened up. The first sentence could now be read to say: "The University will provide ... opposition to ... privacy ... and [opposition to] protection of intellectual property. Looks like a job for semicolons.

Responsibilities
Users will be expected to abide by the policies of the University, whose existence makes the use of these resources available. Every user is also expected to be considerate of the rights of other users. Examples of infringements of these principles may include, but are not limited to, activities that:

"Users will be ..." would be better as "Users are ...".

  • Obstruct or deny access for academic purposes.

This is ambiguous and too narrow. (What if the obstruction is for nonacademic purposes. Is it OK to obstruct nonacademic, but authorized use?). How about: "Substantially and willfully interfere with another person's authorized use."

  • Compromise privacy or confidentiality.
  • Attempt to modify system facilities without authorization, including hardware or software installation.
  • Obstruct the access of others by game playing or monopolizing information resources for entertainment or personal use.
  • Allow unauthorized access to accounts and passwords.
  • Involve unauthorized use of resources for commercial enterprises.
  • Violate intellectual property rights, including copyright and plagiarism.
  • Violate license agreements.

Fine.

An atmosphere of mutual respect among members of the academic community is necessary for the University to function as a center of academic freedom and intellectual advancement. Any use of computing equipment (including posting on electronic bulletin boards, disseminating on Internet web pages, and printing) that violates mutual trust, intimidates or exploits, damages an institution's educational process by undermining the essential freedoms of inquiry and expression constitutes misuse.

I can't parse this last "mutual trust" rule. I don't know what it is trying to say. Even, if I understood what it was trying to say, I don't think anyone could agree on what the rule actually allowed and prohibited. Also, it seems likely that it bans protected speech.

In other words, I think the "mutual trust" rule was much too vague and broad. See ftp://ftp.eff.org/pub/CAF/law/broadrick-v-oklahoma and ftp://ftp.eff.org/pub/CAF/law/naacp-v-button for information about legal requirement for university policies to be clear and narrow. I suggest removing this paragraph. It adds nothing but confusion to the policy.

(Private universities sometimes have broad and subjective honor codes, although, I'm not aware of any that apply only to computer facilities. Public universities much be more objective and clear.)

Individuals using University computing resources will be held responsible for their own actions, and will be subject to local, state, and national laws, and University policies.

This is the "rule rule". It is fine.

Privacy and Security
Network and system administrators are expected to treat the contents of electronic files as private and confidential. However, normal operation and maintenance of the systems requires backup and caching of data and communications, the logging of activity, and the monitoring of general usage patterns.

Since privacy is often defined and judged by situations and circumstances, the University cannot guarantee it. For example, a user alone in an enclosed office has a higher expectation of privacy than a user in an open computer lab where screens are visible to any adjacent user or casual passerby.

In all matters relating to privacy and security of individual accounts and communications, along with requests for release of information, University personnel will abide by appropriate laws, including the Family Education Rights and Privacy Act, or where applicable, the Government Records Access and Management Act. These generally regard student records as private and confidential to most external inquiries. However, they allow for disclosure of student records in response to a proper subpoena or court order from external attorneys, police, and/or administrative agencies.

This is OK.

Though status should not be altered pending action on charges, system administrators are authorized to immediately take actions such as locking accounts when the safety and well-being of students, faculty, or university property is at risk. The Associate Vice-President of Information Technology will then authorize an investigation. The means of investigating will include, but not be limited to, monitoring traffic and files, including the contents thereof.

Investigations should not stay secret forever. I strongly suggest a requirement that subject of an investigation be notified as soon as practical. In some cases this would mean as soon as his or her files are secured. In extreme cases it might be after the investigation.

Complaints and Sanctions
If an issue or dispute cannot be resolved between the respective parties, a written complaint will be processed as follows. Complaints alleging sexual harassment will go to the Associate Vice President for Student Services, for possible forwarding to the Sexual Harassment Committee, if not otherwise resolved. Complaints regarding student violations of the Student Rights and Responsibilities Code will go to the Vice President for Student Services (or delegate). Complaints against faculty will go to the responsible Department Chair, Dean or Provost, with possible forwarding to appropriate bodies in the Faculty Senate. Complaints against computing personnel will be forwarded to the Associate Vice President of Information Technology (or delegate).

The person against whom the complaint is made will be presumed innocent until proven otherwise.

Good.

Sanctions by SUU computing personnel for systems related violations might include admonition, warning, reprimand and/or loss of computing privileges.

This doesn't make sense. It suggest that "computing personnel" can impose sanctions on their own. It also suggests that a student can never be expelled from the university and a professor can never be fired for violating the rules. At most universities, professors are not authorized to impose serious punishments on their own. For example, they can not expel a student from class summarily. I suggest replacing the sentence with:

"Sanctions for violating computer policy can include all regular university sanctions (admonition, warning, reprimand, expulsion, etc.) In addition it can include lose of computer access."

Library Computer Use
As a member of the Federal Depository Library Program (FDLP), the SUU Gerald R. Sherratt Library must provide free and unrestricted searching of information products in the FDLP Electronic Collection. Information access, free of restrictions such as age or residency status, is extended to all other library information sources.

Parents of minors should be aware that in a university setting, serving primarily adults, library personnel are not monitoring what their children may view.

Excellent.
