April 1, 1999

A critique of SUU's new draft policy.   The policy is generally quite good. Minor changes in wording could make it "say what it means" better. It does, however, contains a strange rule against "violating mutual trust". This rule is vague and over-broad and should be removed.

Southern Utah University creates a new draft policy.

March 24, 1999

Churches want to write SUU policy, too. A week ago, the Inter-faith Council of Cedar City asked Southern Utah University postpone a scheduled vote on revising the computer policy.  The churches "are concerned about the impact of this change on the many university students in our churches and religious organizations."  They want to "discuss this matter with the faculty members and students in our churches." They "anticipate providing a policy proposal to [the computer policy subcommittee] by mid-April."

In a critique of the Lewis/Barnes proposal for SUU, I write:  My greatest fear is not that proposal will be accepted, but rather that it will lead to a let's-only-burn-half-the-books "compromise". I hope to show in the rest of this critique that the proposal and the legal arguments that support it are bankrupt and should be given no weight. - Carl Kadie

March 23, 1999

The Censorware Projects reports that when Utah high school students
or adults are banned from a site by the State's Internet "filtering" software, more likely than not, the site was completely innocent. Among the documents banned by Utah: the Holy Bible, the Book of
Mormon, the Declaration of Independence, the United States
Constitution, all of Shakespeare's plays, and The Adventures of
Sheeerlock Holmes.

The front page of the Salt Lake City Tribune has two articles on the Censorware Project report: Internet Filter Used in Schools Blocked the Bible, Salt Lake Tribune, March 23, 1999  &  Not-So-Smart Filter, Salt Lake Tribune, March 23, 1999.

Cyberia, a mailing list about cyberspace law, is discussing the Lewis/Barnes draft alternative to the current SUU policy. An archive is available.

March 22, 1999

This week Southern Utah University will consider several alternatives to their current computer policy. A draft alternative created SUU Professors Timothy B. Lewis and Jeffrey N. Barnes first warns of the "anarchy and violence" that comes from much freedom. The draft then bans sexually explicit material (with a few exceptions). It also requires filtering/censorware. The proposed punishment for a first offense is a one year expulsion from the computer.

March 6, 1999

The Salt Lake Tribune reports: "On Thursday, [Southern Utah University" released a draft of its new Acceptable Use Policy. It is drastically different from the previous version, now allowing SUU students the right to look at virtually anything they want to without fear of punishment." ... "The policy had drawn attention from the federal government and the American Civil Liberties Union, which threatened action if the policy was not changed. "

March 4, 1999

The SUU University Journal reports on press coverage and the "Confessions of a Civilly Disobedient Student". It also says that the administration has been getting much mail against the policy. One email accused the University's president of having a "Big Brother mentality".

The Chronicle o f Higher Education reports on the "Confessions". It also says the chairman of the committee agrees that the policy is overly vague. "The word 'objectionable' shouldn't have been there, there's no doubt about that".

March 2, 1999

Michaun Jensen, the SUU student, confesses "I 'tricked' SUU into enforcing their vague and probably illegal computer policy."  "A speech code that is never enforced can still censor by chilling speech. I hoped that the heat from my civilly disobedient act would melt that chill."

The SUU student newspaper reports that the lab assistant says the Hitler website was not an issue in expelling student from lab. He says she was expelled was partly for adult material but mostly because he thought she wanted to be expelled.

An editorial from the SUU student newspaper from last week says the "computer policy" must be changed.
