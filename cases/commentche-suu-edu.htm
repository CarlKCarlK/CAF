Paul Anthony Gowder <pgowder@LAW.HARVARD.EDU> writes:

>In addition to which, a simple one line "computers shall not be used for
>non-academic purposes" would be sufficient.

Yes, it is strange that a policy that the University spokesman said
was intented to stop nonacademic computer use in computer labs
1. Never mentions computer labs, per se.
2. Never prohibits nonacademic computer use.

Also, I wonder if his concern about nonacademic use of university
resources extends to the library? Are students at SUU also banned from
reading library books for fun? If SUU is really resource poor, then
academic work should certainly be given priority over
recreation. (SUU's own building use policy shows how this can be
done.) Even in a university with substandard resources, however,
academic freedom requires that students and faculty be given wide
discretion in deciding what is "academic". The standard shouldn't be
set by computer staff or other students looking over one's sholders.

As to the problem of sexual harassment in the computer lab: SUU
already has a harassment policy that not only covers harassment via
computers but that also dictates the exact procedure for handling
sexual harassment complaints. (Having a procedure is important because
the line between constitutionally protected expression and unprotected
expression is dim and uncertain.) Computer sites should publicize the
university's sexual harassment rules; they should not try to preempt
them.
For details:
http://www.eff.org/CAF/faq/computer-porn.html
http://www.eff.org/CAF/faq/censorship-and-harassment.html

* * *

Finally, the spokesman insults the U. of Oregon and Idaho State
University by saying the SUU policy was based on them. The U. of
Oregon policy especially, is much better that the SUU policy.
http://cc.uoregon.edu/docs/acceptable_use.html

Perhaps SUU only got the title "Acceptable Use" from the U. of Oregon.
If so, I suggest both schools should change the title of their
policies to the more positive "Computer Policy". This would also be
more consistent with other university policies. For example, schools
generally don't have an "Acceptable Use of the Library Policy"; they
have a "Library Policy". They don't have an "Acceptable Use of the
Student Media Policy"; they have a "Student Media Policy".
